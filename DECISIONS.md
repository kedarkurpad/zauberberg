## Theoretical Relevance

A module qualifies if it passes at least one pillar's test below. Growth, efficiency, or aggregate-welfare metrics with no power, distributional, or conflict dimension are out of scope.

1. **Structural Power & Political Economy** — Strange; Farrell & Newman; Wallerstein; Arrighi; Harvey; Streeck; Mudde; Norris & Inglehart; Petter Törnberg; Anton Törnberg.
   *Test: who holds leverage over production, finance, security, knowledge, or discourse structures?*
2. **Distributional Justice** — Piketty; Milanovic.
   *Test: who gains or loses across a distribution, not just in aggregate?*
3. **Peace and Conflict** — Galtung; Fanon; Jones (structural violence in disability/mental-health policy); Bacevich; Toft & Kushi.
   *Test: does this reveal direct/structural violence, or the blowback cost of a dominant power's policy?*
4. **Energy** — Klein; Riofrancos; Malm; Mitchell.
   *Test: does this show energy infrastructure, extraction, or transition politics as contested power, not just supply-and-demand economics?*

**Value-polarity exception on the ticker (added 2026-09-11):** by default, a ticker delta is colored by its indicator's assigned series/pillar token, not by whether the move is "good" or "bad" \u2014 up/down isn't uniformly positive or negative across most indicators (e.g. the Treasury spread, the dollar index). Three indicators are a deliberate exception, carrying an explicit `polarity` field because this project's own distributive-justice framing (Piketty; Milanovic) treats their direction as normatively loaded: `labor-share` (`good-up` \u2014 rising labor share reads as good) and `wealth-share-top1` / `income-share-top1-us` (`bad-up` \u2014 rising top-1% concentration reads as bad). Their deltas render turquoise (good) or ochre (bad), **universally, regardless of which pillar the indicator otherwise belongs to** \u2014 updated same day from an earlier sage/ristra version. Tradeoff: ochre is also `spr-level`'s plain Pillar-4 identity color, so a neutral SPR move and a "bad" judgment on either top-1%-share indicator render in the identical ochre for unrelated reasons \u2014 accepted, not an oversight. Turquoise has no such collision today (unclaimed by any live, non-polarity indicator). No other indicator should get a `polarity` field without equally clear, stated normative grounding; most of this project's metrics (yield spreads, currency indices, reserve levels) don't have one.

**Energy module color-token exception (added 2026-09-11):** the generation-mix stacked-area chart inside the Energy (Pillar 4) panel uses the full 5-token palette as a plain categorical set (one color per fuel-type series: fossil/nuclear/renewables), not per-pillar identity, since a single ochre token can't distinguish 3 series. Scoped to charts internal to this one module panel only; the ticker's existing one-token-per-pillar rule (and its known ochre collision between `spr-level` and the two `bad-up` polarity indicators) is unchanged elsewhere. (This exception originally also covered a crude-import treemap that sat alongside the generation-mix chart; that treemap was removed 2026-09-11 by request — see changelog — so the exception is now scoped to a single chart.)

**Placeholder panel titles (added 2026-09-11, SUPERSEDED 2026-09-13):** by request, the four pillar panel headings were changed from full names to plain numerals (1–4), preserved for screen readers via `aria-label`. This was explicitly flagged to "revisit once real panel content ships" — see the next entry for that revisit.

Each surviving panel gets a plain-language section header **outside** the `panel-placeholder` box (not inside it as the numeral was), plus an unwired "See all →" placeholder link below the panel, styled like the topbar's existing disabled nav links. Mapping: Distributional Justice → **"Inequality"**; Peace and Conflict → **"Migration"**; Energy → **"Climate"**. Tradeoffs, accepted by request rather than overlooked: "Migration" is narrower than the full Peace and Conflict pillar (which also covers non-migration blowback/structural-violence framing per Galtung/Bacevich/Toft & Kushi), and "Climate" is narrower than the Energy pillar's actual test (leverage/transition politics, not a climate frame specifically). The underlying pillar tests and code are unchanged — this only changes the visible label.

**Panel removed: Structural Power / student loans (added 2026-09-13, by request, "for now"):** the Structural Power & Political Economy panel (student loans owned & securitized, Pillar 1) is no longer shown in the layout, to avoid a fourth, unlabeled-pillar panel confusing the new three-header layout above. Bringing the panel back later is re-adding its markup + the one function call, not rebuilding the pipeline. **Ticker "see all indicators" link (added 2026-09-13):** an unwired placeholder link sits directly under the ticker bar, same disabled-link convention as the topbar's Theory Notes/Methodology/About. Not sticky itself — only the ticker bar above it pins on scroll.

Revisit with a wide-viewport max-width if that turns out to matter in practice. ## Accessibility

- Baseline: WCAG 2.1 AA (4.5:1 contrast for body text, 3:1 for large text/UI components).
- Typefaces are system-font stacks only (no Google Fonts or other externally hosted faces), for cross-platform rendering and low-network reliability; tradeoff is losing the more distinctive custom pairing (previously Space Grotesk / IBM Plex Mono) in favor of default OS faces.

## Technical Requirements

- Computational social science / NLP methods (sentiment and discourse analysis on social media data) are part of the toolkit, applied to drivers of far-right and ethnonationalist political outcomes — theory anchored in Pillar 1 above (Mudde; Norris & Inglehart; Petter & Anton Törnberg). Likely output form: sentiment time-series by electoral cycle and/or discourse-community network graphs, not single aggregate scores.
- **Hosting: GitHub Pages** (static only — no server, no client-side secrets).
- **Data pipeline architecture: scheduled GitHub Action → static JSON, not live client-side polling.** A cron-scheduled GH Action fetches each source server-side (API keys live in repo secrets), computes the display value, and commits a `ticker-data.json`; the page fetches that JSON at load. Chosen over direct browser-to-API calls because several sources require a key that can't be exposed client-side in a public repo (FRED, BLS) and/or block cross-origin requests. Tradeoff: data is only as fresh as the last Action run, not truly real-time.
- **Ticker indicator tiering, by lift/access/refresh cadence:**
  - *Tier 1 (build first):* FRED T10Y2Y (free key, daily), BLS Black–White unemployment gap (free key, monthly), GDELT global tone (keyless DOC 2.0 `timelinetone` endpoint).
  - *Tier 2:* ECB SDW BTP–Bund spread (keyless SDMX, daily, two-series parse); OFAC SDN additions (public CSV, but "rolling 90-day additions" requires diffing snapshots collected going forward — undercounts for the first ~90 days after launch, cold-start tradeoff); EU ETS carbon price sourced from Ember Climate's open API rather than ICE Endex, since ICE Endex data is licensed/paid — changes the footer's stated source for that indicator.
  - *Kept as topline indicators despite annual-only refresh:* Global Top 1% Wealth Share (WID.world) and US Gini Coefficient (Census Bureau/ACS). Both have real, freely-accessible APIs; they just update once a year, so their ticker value will sit static between releases — accepted tradeoff to keep long-run distributional-inequality signal visible on the ticker.
  - *Dropped from v1 (no viable low-lift API found):* Cornell/Illinois Labor Action Tracker (data lives only in an interactive map, no public API); ACLED conflict count (now gated behind org-level registration and OAuth-token tiers, with redistribution restrictions — heavier lift than fits "lightest lift").
  - *Dropped by request:* GDELT tone sample. It worked in principle (keyless DOC 2.0 API) but proved unreliable from GitHub's runners (persistent network-level failures even after retries and UA-header fixes), and — independent of that reliability issue — the project would rather give its own NLP/sentiment-analysis panels (see the computational-social-science bullet above) more visual prominence than a borrowed proxy tone metric on the ticker.
  - *Added, reusing already-established APIs/secrets (no new plumbing):* Labor Share of GDP (FRED, Penn World Table series, annual — chosen over a BLS quarterly index alternative whose units weren't unambiguously a % share); White\u2013Black Median Household Income Gap (Census ACS, same pattern as the existing Gini fetch, same secret); US Top 1% Income Share, Before Tax (OWID/WID, same CSV-parsing code as the existing global wealth-share indicator, generalized to take a chart slug + entity name). All three logged as Pillar 1/2 fits (structural power over production and income, distributional justice).
  - *Added 2026-09-11 (forex, no new secret):* Nominal Broad U.S. Dollar Index (FRED, `DTWEXBGS`, daily) as a Pillar 1 indicator — trade-weighted dollar strength as a proxy for currency/finance structural power (Strange; also the dollar-clearing infrastructure Farrell & Newman's "weaponized interdependence" concerns), not a bare FX quote. Reuses the existing `FRED_API_KEY`.
  - *Added 2026-09-11 (energy, new secret):* Strategic Petroleum Reserve crude oil ending stocks (EIA, series `PET.WCSSTUS1.W`, weekly) as a Pillar 4 indicator — a held strategic reserve is energy security as state leverage (Mitchell, *Carbon Democracy*), not a bare commodity price, so it clears the Pillar 4 relevance test that a raw oil/gas spot price would not. Requires a new `EIA_API_KEY` secret (free registration; EIA has no unauthenticated fallback the way BLS/Census do). Visual token: ochre, shared with the still-unbuilt EU ETS carbon-price indicator under the one-token-per-pillar rule (both Pillar 4).
  - *Added 2026-09-11 (energy, leverage framing, no new secret):* Energy Price Volatility — WTI 20-Day Realized Vol (FRED `DCOILWTICO`, daily) as a Pillar 4 indicator. Deliberately reports the *volatility* of the WTI spot price (annualized stdev of daily log returns over a trailing 20-trading-day window), not the price level — a bare commodity price would fail the Pillar 4 relevance test the same way DECISIONS.md already excludes one for `spr-level`. Sharp price swings are read as exposure to supply-chain disruption and geopolitical leverage over energy infrastructure (Klein; Riofrancos; Malm; Mitchell), which is the leverage framing requested — as distinct from a distributional framing (energy cost burden by income group), which was considered and not used here. Reuses the existing `FRED_API_KEY`. Visual token: ochre, same Pillar-4 token as `spr-level` (already an accepted collision under the one-token-per-pillar rule; adding a second ochre item doesn't create a new one, just extends the existing shared token to a third context). Unverified against a live FRED response (no network egress in this build environment) — same caveat convention as `fetchSPR`/`fetchGenerationMix`/`fetchGiniSeries`; the volatility math (window size, log-return convention, annualization factor) additionally hasn't been sanity-checked against an independent source and should be spot-checked on the first real run.
  - *Added 2026-09-11 (housing, distributional framing, no new secret):* US Home Price YoY Growth (FRED `CSUSHPISA`, S&P/Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index, SA, monthly) as a Pillar 2 indicator. A bare index level (base Jan 2000=100) would be both uninformative and would read as plain aggregate-welfare/supply-and-demand economics, which the Theoretical Relevance section excludes — so the ticker instead surfaces year-over-year appreciation, framed per Piketty's capital-appreciation logic: home-price gains accrue to existing owners as asset wealth while pricing out renters/non-owners, so a faster YoY rate reads as a faster-widening asset-wealth gap. The ticker's `change` field is accordingly the month-over-month shift in that YoY rate (acceleration/deceleration of the gap), not a plain index-point diff — a distinct convention from every other indicator on the ticker, logged here so it isn't mistaken for an error. Two framings were considered (Pillar 1/Harvey: housing financialization as structural power; Pillar 2/Piketty: asset-wealth inequality); Piketty/Pillar 2 was chosen by request. Deliberately given NO `polarity` field, unlike `wealth-share-top1`/`income-share-top1-us`: those measure top-1% concentration directly, where "up = bad" is unambiguous; a national home-price index instead reflects gains across a broad (~65%) homeowner population with genuinely mixed effects (existing owners gain, renters/prospective buyers lose), which doesn't meet the "equally clear, stated normative grounding" bar the polarity exception itself sets. Reuses the existing `FRED_API_KEY`. Visual token: sage, matching every other Pillar 2 ticker indicator under the one-token-per-pillar rule. Unverified against a live FRED response (no network egress in this build environment) — same caveat convention as the other FRED fetchers; also flagging that FRED has a separate NSA series (`CSUSHPINSA`) that must not be substituted for `CSUSHPISA`, or the YoY figure would reabsorb seasonal noise the SA series is meant to remove.
  - *Added 2026-09-11 (peace and conflict, no new secret):* Forcibly Displaced Persons \u2014 Global Total (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder API, `population` endpoint, annual) as a Pillar 3 indicator \u2014 the sum of refugees, asylum-seekers, IDPs, and other people in need of international protection (UNHCR's own "forcibly displaced" definition), read as the human toll of direct/structural violence and dominant-power blowback (Galtung; Fanon), not a bare migration count. Chosen over ACLED (already dropped from v1, org-gated OAuth tiers) and UCDP's GED API (free, but needs its own token) since UNHCR's population API is fully keyless \u2014 no registration at all, no new secret. Deliberately given NO `polarity` field for now: a rising count arguably clears the "equally clear, stated normative grounding" bar more easily than `housing-price-index` did, but every polarity addition so far was added by explicit request for a named indicator, and this wasn't \u2014 flagging it here as a candidate rather than deciding it unilaterally. Takes the ristra token, the first live ticker use of the color already reserved for Pillar 3 (see the `fetchBls` comment in fetch-ticker-data.mjs). Unverified against a live response (no network egress in this build environment) \u2014 same caveat convention as `fetchSPR`/`fetchGenerationMix`/`fetchGiniSeries`.
  - *Data-staleness finding (2026-09-11):* `labor-share`'s client-side fallback value was a made-up placeholder (62.8%/+0.1pp) that didn't match reality — verified directly against FRED that the real latest observation is 2023 at 56.83% (down from 57.62% in 2022), corrected to 56.8%/-0.8pp. More importantly, FRED lists this series' Next Release Date as **Not Available**, meaning Penn World Table 11.0 may never get another scheduled update — a materially bigger staleness risk than the "refreshes annually" framing used elsewhere for annual indicators. If the live fetch keeps returning 2023 indefinitely, that's the source being stuck, not a bug. Separately confirmed the OWID/WID top-1%-share dataset (`wealth-share-top1`, `income-share-top1-us`) runs through 2024 with a next-expected-update of June 2027 — meaningfully fresher than labor-share, though the exact current point values for the "World" and "United States" entities weren't individually verified (would require a full-dataset fetch); those two indicators' fallback numbers remain illustrative placeholders pending a real fetch run.

## Structural Power & Political Economy module visualizations (Pillar 1)

- **What/why (added 2026-09-11):** replaced Panel 1's Lorem-ipsum placeholder with a line chart of Student Loans Owned and Securitized (FRED `SLOASM`, sourced from the Federal Reserve Board's G.19 Consumer Credit release, monthly, millions of USD, NSA). Relevance test: the balance itself \u2014 debt that has been packaged and is *held* as a claim by financial institutions \u2014 is the leverage/structural-power signal (Strange; Harvey), the same way the ticker's `spr-level` indicator's bare reserve level (not a derived rate) already counts as its own Pillar 4 leverage signal. No YoY/ratio transformation was needed to clear the relevance bar, unlike `housing-price-index` or `energy-price-volatility`, because the level *is* the signal here, not a proxy needing a rate-of-change wrapper.
- **Secondary framing considered:** the request that prompted this ("more owned/securitized debt \u2192 more social control, less mobility") also reads as Galtung's structural-violence framing (Pillar 3 \u2014 constrained life chances). Pillar 1 was chosen as primary since "owned and securitized" specifically names the leverage-holder dimension the Pillar 1 test asks about ("who holds leverage over production, finance..."); Pillar 3 is logged here as the effect/secondary reading, not discarded.
- **Data quirk:** despite being nominally a "monthly" series, `SLOASM` only actually populates Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec (other months return "." from FRED) \u2014 confirmed via a live fetch of the FRED series and data-table pages on 2026-09-11. The fetcher filters the empty months out; this is the source's own reporting cadence, not a bug.
- **New chart type reuse:** a single-series line chart, same chart type as the Distributional Justice Gini chart (one continuous scalar over time, not a breakdown \u2014 no categorical-palette exception needed). Colored terracotta, matching every other Pillar 1 ticker indicator (`treasury-spread`, `labor-share`, `dollar-index`) under the one-token-per-pillar rule. Unlike the Gini chart's min/max-padded axis, this chart anchors its value axis at 0 \u2014 a debt-level series read against a 0 baseline is the more honest picture of how much the balance has actually grown since 2006; a padded axis would visually flatten that growth.
- **Output file:** a new sibling file `student-loan-data.json` (same pattern as `energy-data.json`/`gini-data.json`), written by the same scheduled GitHub Action, same fall-back-to-last-published behavior on fetch failure. `update-ticker-data.yml` now commits it alongside the other three output files.
- **Caveat, narrower than usual:** unlike `fetchSPR`/`fetchGenerationMix`/`fetchGiniSeries`, this fetcher's series ID and JSON response shape *were* confirmed against a live FRED fetch on 2026-09-11 (both the series page and the full data table), so it does not carry the "unverified against a live response" caveat those three do. Reuses the existing `FRED_API_KEY` \u2014 no new secret. The page's fallback array uses real historical FRED values (December of each year, 2006\u20132025, plus the latest available point, Jun 2026) rather than Lorem placeholders, consistent with the corrected `labor-share` and `gini-data.json` fallback conventions.
- **Rendering bug found and fixed (2026-09-12):** the first screenshot of this chart against live data showed the last two x-axis year labels overlapping into garbled text. Root cause: the shared "every Nth label, plus always show first/last" logic (`labelStep = round(n/8)`) can land a modulo-triggered label within a couple of indices of the forced last point once `n` is large \u2014 the live SLOASM series (quarterly since 2006) has ~80 points, denser than the ~21-point fallback array this logic was eyeballed against. Fixed by suppressing any modulo-triggered label that falls within a full `labelStep` of the last index, guaranteeing a minimum pixel gap independent of series length. The Distributional Justice (Gini) chart below shares the identical labeling code and got the same fix, even though its shorter annual series hadn't visibly collided yet.

## Peace and Conflict module visualizations (Pillar 3)

- **What/why (added 2026-09-11):** replaced Panel 3's Lorem-ipsum placeholder with a grouped bar chart of forcibly displaced persons by UNHCR-defined region of origin, latest available year (UNHCR Refugee Data Finder API). This is the dashboard's first non-time-series panel chart: a cross-sectional snapshot of where displacement is concentrated is itself the leverage/blowback signal (Galtung; Fanon) for this module, so a trend line wasn't the right shape here the way it was for Panels 1 and 2.
- **Region bucketing:** built at fetch time from the API's own `/countries` endpoint (country \u2192 UNHCR region), not a hardcoded country list, so the grouping doesn't go stale if regional classifications change. Unverified against a live response \u2014 same caveat as the ticker indicator above; the region field name is per UNHCR's documented schema but hasn't been confirmed against a real payload.
- **Shape-mismatch finding (2026-09-11):** the first real Action run published a chart with only one region ("Southern Asia," 7.0M) instead of a real global breakdown \u2014 exactly the risk the "unverified against a live response" caveat flagged. Root cause not yet confirmed (couldn't get far enough into UNHCR's SDK-heavy API docs page to see the `/population/` endpoint's real response schema), so the field-name guesses in `fetchDisplacementByRegion()` (`r.coo`, `r.refugees`, etc.) are still unconfirmed. Added two guardrails rather than a real fix: (1) diagnostic `console.log` lines printing the raw first row and row count from both `/countries` and `/population/`, so the next Action run's logs show the actual shape; (2) a sanity check that treats fewer than 3 nonzero regions as a failure (falls back to last-published/placeholder) instead of publishing a misleadingly sparse chart labeled "live." Same pair of guardrails (diagnostic log + a [50M, 300M] plausibility band) added to the ticker-level `fetchDisplacement()`, since it shares the same unconfirmed field names and hadn't been independently checked. Next step: pull the Action's log output (or hit the two URLs directly, since the API is keyless) to confirm the real field names and fix the mapping for real.
- **Root cause confirmed, fixed (2026-09-11):** the [diag] logs from the first real run showed the field names (`region`, `coo`, `refugees`, etc.) were correct all along \u2014 the real bug was UNHCR's API silently capping both endpoints at its default page size (100 rows), compounded in `fetchDisplacement()` by `coo_all=false&coa_all=false` not aggregating server-side into one global row the way the docs implied. Fix: `fetchDisplacement()` now uses the same `coo_all=true&coa_all=false` per-origin-country shape `fetchDisplacementByRegion()` already used, and both fetchers now request `limit=1000` and sum every row client-side. Diagnostic logging left in place, repurposed to confirm whether `limit=1000` actually raises UNHCR's cap \u2014 if row counts are still ~100 on the next run, the cap is server-enforced and real `&page=N` pagination is the follow-up, not a further field-name chase.
- **This diagnosis was itself wrong \u2014 corrected 2026-09-12:** the ticker's `forcibly-displaced` indicator came back at 15.0M against a real ~120M+ global figure. Root cause: UNHCR's own docs state a dimension "if not specified... will be summed and aggregated to one row" \u2014 aggregation happens on OMITTING `coo`/`coa`, not on passing `coo_all=true&coa_all=false` as the fix above did. `coo_all=true` does the opposite of aggregate: it explicitly breaks out every origin country as its own row. So `fetchDisplacement()` was fetching one row per (origin, destination) COUNTRY PAIR \u2014 there are far more than 1000 of those across a 3-year window \u2014 and summing only the first 1000 undercounted by ~8x; `limit=1000` never raised a server-side cap, because there was never a cap to raise. Real fix: `fetchDisplacement()` now omits `coo`/`coo_all`/`coa`/`coa_all` entirely, so both dimensions aggregate server-side into one row per year, exactly as documented. `fetchDisplacementByRegion()` keeps `coo_all=true` (it genuinely wants the per-origin breakdown) but now omits `coa`/`coa_all` entirely too, so each origin country's row already sums across every destination server-side, rather than relying on `limit=1000` client-side summation happening to cover enough pair-rows \u2014 it usually did, by coincidence of `250-ish` countries times a handful of major destinations per country landing under 1000, but was never guaranteed to.
- **Output file:** a new sibling file `peace-data.json` (same pattern as `energy-data.json`/`gini-data.json`/`student-loan-data.json`), written by the same scheduled GitHub Action, same fall-back-to-last-published behavior on fetch failure. `update-ticker-data.yml` now commits it alongside the other output files.
- **Color:** ristra throughout, matching the ticker indicator above and the color already reserved for Pillar 3.

## Distributional Justice module visualizations (Pillar 2)

## Discourse-tagging module (Pillar 1)

- **Method choice (2026-09-12, by request):** the footer's "Discourse-tagging output format" module — previously fully synthetic placeholder cards — is now backed by lexicon-based scoring rather than an LLM call. Considered and rejected: (1) a paid Anthropic API call, and (2) a locally-run open-weight classifier (transformers.js/onnxruntime-node, no API needed). Chosen instead: plain word-frequency matching against two established academic dictionaries — the Moral Foundations Dictionary (Graham, Haidt & Nosek: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, purity) and an NRC-style emotion lexicon (Mohammad & Turney convention: anger, fear, joy, sadness, plus positive/negative tone). Reasoning, by the three criteria requested — interpretability, setup lift, and credibility for a junior-researcher-scoped demo: every score is an auditable word count against a citable, peer-recognized dictionary; there's no model runtime, API key, or rate limit to manage; and using established lexicons with their known limits stated up front reads as more methodologically sound than an unvalidated model call.
- **Explicit tradeoff, per Törnberg's own typology (the paper that motivated this module):** this is second-generation NLP — dictionary/word-frequency matching — not the third-generation, context-sensitive "Theory-Driven AI Analysis" framing floated earlier in this project's design discussion. It won't catch sarcasm, novel metaphor, or phrasing outside the dictionary. The module is labeled "lexicon-based moral/emotion scoring" in the UI, not TDAA, so the framing matches the actual method.
- **Starter subset, not the full dictionaries:** the word lists bundled in `fetch-ticker-data.mjs` are a small illustrative subset (~10-12 words per category, with `word*` prefix wildcards mirroring the real dictionaries' own convention), not the full published MFD 2.0 / NRC EmoLex files. Flagged in-code as a pending upgrade before this is a real research instrument rather than a demo.
- **Source:** GovInfo Congressional Record (CREC) API — official daily floor-speech transcripts, fitting the already-logged input scope ("public speech transcripts... not private citizens' social media"). Three-step fetch: `collections/CREC` for the latest package → `packages/{id}/granules` filtered to House/Senate speech classes → per-granule `htm` text, stripped and scored. Unverified against a live response (no network egress in this build environment) — same caveat convention as `fetchSPR`/`fetchGenerationMix`/`fetchGiniSeries`; the field names (`packageId`, `granuleId`, `dateIssued`) are per GovInfo's documented structure but unconfirmed.
- **New secret, optional:** `GOVINFO_API_KEY`. GovInfo/api.data.gov accepts the shared, unregistered `DEMO_KEY` with no signup at all, at a low shared rate limit (30/hr, 50/day) — the fetcher defaults to it, so no new secret is strictly required. Register a personal free key only if that quota proves too tight for the daily schedule.
- **Color-token exception:** the five moral-foundation categories reuse the existing five-token palette as a plain categorical set (one color per category), the same reasoning already accepted for the Energy module's generation-mix chart — a single Pillar-1 token can't distinguish five categories. Scoped to this module's tags only; the ticker's one-token-per-pillar rule is unaffected elsewhere.
- **Output file:** a new sibling file `discourse-data.json` (same pattern as `peace-data.json`/`gini-data.json`/etc.), written by the same scheduled GitHub Action, same fall-back-to-last-published behavior on fetch failure. `update-ticker-data.yml` now commits it — and, while editing that file, also fixed a pre-existing gap where `peace-data.json` had never been added to the commit step since the Peace and Conflict module shipped.
- **Shape-mismatch finding and fix (2026-09-12):** the first real Action run returned an HTTP 400 from the `collections` call. Root cause: GovInfo deprecated the plain `offset` query param in favor of `offsetMark` (must start at `*`), which the original write-up omitted entirely, and separately assumed a `dateIssued` field on the collections response that doesn't exist there (only `packageId`/`lastModified`/`packageLink` do — confirmed against GPO's own sample responses). Fixed by adding `offsetMark=*` to both the `collections` and `granules` calls and parsing the package date out of the `packageId` string (`CREC-YYYY-MM-DD`) instead. Also dropped an unconfirmed `granuleClass=HOUSE&granuleClass=SENATE` query filter on the granules call — not a documented parameter for that endpoint — in favor of a client-side filter that degrades gracefully if the expected class-like field isn't present on a live response.
- **UI change (2026-09-12, by request):** the footer cards previously showed a ~220-character prose excerpt of the source speech. Replaced with the actual matched dictionary words for the entry's dominant moral-foundation and dominant emotion category (e.g. "Matched terms — Authority: law, duty · Fear: threat, danger"), or an explicit "no category cleared the minimum-match threshold" message when nothing qualifies. `scoreText()` in `fetch-ticker-data.mjs` now returns up to 5 distinct matched words per category (`matchedKeywords`) alongside the existing rate/count fields; the raw text excerpt is still written to `discourse-data.json` for provenance but is no longer the card's primary display. This is a better fit for the module's own stated goal (interpretability): showing the literal dictionary hits is more auditable than a paragraph of surrounding prose, and keeps the card's claim scoped to what a lexicon match can actually support.
- **"Vibe of the Congress" trailing-days redesign (2026-09-13, by request):** superseding the "no category cleared" filler cards above — `fetchDiscourseTags()` now walks backward through CREC packages one calendar day at a time (up to a 21-day lookback) and keeps at most one threshold-clearing entry per day, targeting a trailing 4. A day whose candidates don't clear `MIN_MATCHES` is skipped entirely rather than surfaced as a "below threshold" card, so the loop naturally falls back to the nearest earlier qualifying day when asked to reach 4 — by explicit request, over the alternative of just showing fewer than 4 on a sparse week. The 4 returned entries can therefore span more than 4 calendar days. Each entry now carries a `date` field (the CREC package's own date), surfaced in the UI as a small per-card label. `index.html` defensively slices to 4 client-side, but the real limiting is server-side. New assumption flagged as unverified: this redesign is the first time the `collections/CREC` response's package ordering (assumed most-recent-first, carried over from the original single-package logic) actually matters across multiple days — confirm via the new `[diag]` log lines on the first live multi-day run.
